FRAME01 / 34

The transformation matrix

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Every AI initiative arrives labelled "transformation", so the portfolio has no shape and every pitch sounds the same.

THE MOVE

Two axes: focus (process or strategic) and magnitude (incremental or transformational). Four quadrants: Optimize, Enhance, Revolutionize, Transform. Place every initiative before you fund any.

Place it before you fund it.

FRAME02 / 34

The Optimize trap

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Cost-saving automation is easy to approve and easy to measure, so the whole portfolio quietly clusters in one quadrant.

THE MOVE

Check the matrix for gaps. Most organisations cluster in Optimize. Strategic leaders balance across all four.

An all-Optimize portfolio is a cost program, not a strategy.

FRAME03 / 34

Enhance is the quiet middle

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Enhancement — better products, personalised service — sits between the measurable and the visionary, and gets skipped in both directions.

THE MOVE

Name at least one Enhance initiative deliberately. Recommendations, personalised service, AI-assisted delivery: they compound with what you already sell.

Improve what you sell, not just how you run.

FRAME04 / 34

Revolutionize needs more than a pilot budget

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Reinventing a core process gets scoped like a software project — and dies at pilot scale.

THE MOVE

If a quadrant-3 play (process, transformational) is on the table, fund it as a program with executive sponsorship, not as a pilot with a pilot's budget.

Reinvention at pilot budget is theatre.

FRAME05 / 34

The multiplier test

WHY IT GOES WRONG

AI gets costed as a replacement for labour hours, and the case collapses at the first budget review.

THE MOVE

Ask what AI multiplies: data, reach, judgement, throughput. Fund what it multiplies, not just what it replaces.

AI scales whatever is fed into it, including the flaws.

FOUNDATIONS06 / 34

The data value pyramid

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Ambitions get set at the top of the pyramid while the data sits at the bottom, and no one wrote down the distance.

THE MOVE

Five levels: collection, integration, descriptive, predictive, prescriptive. Locate the organisation honestly, then locate each ambition.

Locate yourself before you schedule the future.

FOUNDATIONS07 / 34

You cannot skip levels

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Leadership sees prescriptive demos and asks for Level 5 outcomes from Level 2 data.

THE MOVE

Map every ambition to a level, and fund the levels underneath it. Predictive needs descriptive; prescriptive needs predictive.

You cannot skip levels.

FOUNDATIONS08 / 34

Integration before intelligence

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Point solutions get bought while the data sits in disconnected systems, and every pilot starts by rebuilding the same joins.

THE MOVE

Customer 360, unified inventory, one view of operations: fund integration as an AI initiative in its own right.

Connected data is the first AI product.

FOUNDATIONS09 / 34

Descriptive is not done

WHY IT GOES WRONG

"We have dashboards" gets counted as analytics maturity, and the gap to predictive stays invisible.

THE MOVE

Dashboards describe; they don't predict. The move from "what happened" to "what will happen" is a modelling discipline, not a reporting upgrade.

'What happened' is a report. 'What will happen' is a different investment.

FOUNDATIONS10 / 34

The honest level score

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Data readiness gets scored by the team asking for the budget.

THE MOVE

Score data readiness 0–10 per initiative, with evidence. Below 7: stop, and fund data infrastructure first.

Under 7 is a data project wearing an AI costume.

FOUNDATIONS11 / 34

Garbage in, multiplied

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Data quality problems look survivable at reporting scale and become existential at decision scale.

THE MOVE

Fix the quality of whatever feeds the model before the model feeds the decisions.

AI scales whatever is fed into it, including the flaws.

BALANCE12 / 34

Three horizons

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The portfolio gets filled with this year's payback projects, and the 3-to-5-year capabilities never get funded.

THE MOVE

H1: optimise the core — 70%. H2: build emerging business — 20%. H3: create the future — 10%. Adjust for industry, but hold all three.

All H1 is no future. All H3 is no present.

BALANCE13 / 34

The over-rotation warnings

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The allocation drifts every budget cycle, and nobody notices until it's lopsided.

THE MOVE

Check the drift annually: too much H1 means short-term thinking; too much H3 means burning cash on uncertain futures.

Watch the drift, not just the split.

BALANCE14 / 34

H1 pays for H3

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Horizons get funded as competitors rather than as a system.

THE MOVE

The measurable wins fund the emerging bets. Say it out loud when H1 business cases are approved: part of the return is H3 budget.

The core funds the future.

BALANCE15 / 34

H3 is an option, not a line item

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Transformational bets get judged by H1 ROI maths and killed by the wrong test.

THE MOVE

Value H3 as option value: the right to move fast if the technology or the market turns. Small, patient, watched.

H3 buys the right to move fast later.

INVEST16 / 34

The four value categories

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Business cases count only cost reduction, so every initiative competes on the same narrow axis.

THE MOVE

Total value = cost reduction + revenue growth + risk reduction + strategic positioning. Price all four, or the case is wrong before the maths starts.

Four categories, or the case is wrong before the maths.

INVEST17 / 34

The ROI thresholds

WHY IT GOES WRONG

One ROI bar gets applied across horizons, so H3 bets die on an H1 test.

THE MOVE

H1: above 200% in year one. H2: above 150% over three years. H3: above 100% over five years — or a strategic must-have. Low confidence in value raises the bar; high certainty lowers it.

Match the threshold to the horizon.

INVEST18 / 34

The five-year cost

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Year-one cost gets approved and the ongoing bill — model upkeep, retraining, monitoring — arrives as a surprise.

THE MOVE

Continuous learning and upkeep run 30–50% of build cost annually. Calculate five-year TCO, not year-one cost.

Calculate the five-year cost, not the year-one cost.

INVEST19 / 34

Accuracy against risk

WHY IT GOES WRONG

A 90% accurate system sounds excellent until you ask what the other 10% touches.

THE MOVE

Set the accuracy bar by consequence: low stakes 70–80% can be fine; regulated or high-consequence work needs 95–99% and the oversight to match.

The bar is set by consequence, not by the demo.

INVEST20 / 34

Explainability costs

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Regulated decisions get built on models nobody can explain, and the gap appears at audit.

THE MOVE

If the decision is regulated or client-facing, budget 15–20% more for explainability — before the build, not during the audit.

Budget the explanation with the build.

INVEST21 / 34

The two red flags

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Scores look clean while the two failures that actually kill projects sit outside the scoring.

THE MOVE

Automatic override for: automating a human touchpoint with no theory of why it exists, and no validated problem owner. Either flag stops the funding, whatever the ROI.

A polished demo is not evidence that anyone needs it.

INVEST22 / 34

Highest ROI is not the best investment

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The ranking by ROI gets treated as the decision, and the projects with AI-specific red flags win on a technicality.

THE MOVE

Score traditional criteria and AI-specific criteria together — data readiness, learning cost, accuracy against risk, explainability, ethics. Then let the red flags veto.

Highest ROI ≠ best AI investment.

INVEST23 / 34

One biased system, millions of biased decisions

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Ethics gets assessed per decision instead of per system.

THE MOVE

Before funding, ask what the system decides at scale, who it treats differently, and who is accountable when it does. Price the ethics work into the case.

Unethical AI is worse than no AI.

ADOPT24 / 34

The five phases

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Adoption gets planned as one big rollout, skipping the phases where the learning happens.

THE MOVE

Knowledge building, pilot experimentation, selective deployment, scaling operations, comprehensive transformation. Know the phase you are in before you fund the next.

Name the phase before you fund the phase.

ADOPT25 / 34

Most fail by skipping

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The visible successes are at Phase 4, so everyone plans for Phase 4.

THE MOVE

Most organisations fail by trying to skip to scaling. Respect the journey; fund the phase you are in.

Most fail by skipping to Phase 4.

ADOPT26 / 34

The budget shape changes

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Pilots get tiny budgets, so pilots stay tiny; scaling gets huge budgets, so scaling starts before the pilots taught anything.

THE MOVE

Budget by phase: 1–5% knowledge, 10–20% pilots, 30–40% selective deployment, 40–50% scaling, then sustained. The shape is the plan.

The budget curve is the adoption plan.

ADOPT27 / 34

Pilots are for learning, not for proving

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Pilots get run as mini-rollouts to prove the decision was right.

THE MOVE

A pilot's deliverable is what was learned: where it worked, where it broke, what the scale would need. Write that down, or the pilot was a demo.

A pilot that only proves is a demo.

ADOPT28 / 34

The centre of excellence arrives at scaling

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The CoE gets stood up first, becomes a bottleneck, and owns standards for work it has never run.

THE MOVE

Build the CoE from people who ran successful pilots, at the scaling phase — not before.

The CoE is built from pilots, not from PowerPoint.

DECIDE29 / 34

Criteria before pitches

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The weighting gets chosen after the pitches, by people who have already favourites.

THE MOVE

Agree the weighting criteria before the first pitch, and give the AI-specific criteria real weight — half or more. Then score every pitch against the same sheet.

Criteria after pitches are verdicts, not criteria.

DECIDE30 / 34

The designed dilemma

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The investment meeting becomes an ROI beauty contest, and the highest-ROI project with the red flag wins by default.

THE MOVE

Construct the agenda so the highest-ROI option carries a real AI-specific flag — data prep, ethics, budget. If the dilemma doesn't exist, create it: that decision is the practice.

Traditional criteria would fund the wrong projects.

DECIDE31 / 34

Scale, pivot, kill

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Pilots drift into production or die quietly; both outcomes skip the decision.

THE MOVE

At the gate: scale on ROI trajectory, feasibility, market readiness, fit and resources; pivot if close but the conditions changed; kill if it missed. Killing is a decision, made on purpose.

Scale/Pivot/Kill is a framework, not a formula.

DECIDE32 / 34

Fast-follow is a strategy

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Every competitive AI threat triggers a pioneer response, at pioneer cost and pioneer risk.

THE MOVE

Match the response to the horizon: pioneer (H3), fast-follow (H2), wait and watch (H1). Fast-follow with a funded pathway is often the rational answer.

You don't have to be first. You have to be ready.

DECIDE33 / 34

The five questions

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Approvals happen on slides, and the five questions that matter never get asked out loud.

THE MOVE

Before any approval: what problem, whose problem, what data, what accuracy against what risk, who owns the outcome. If the pitch can't answer all five, it isn't ready.

If they can't answer all five, you're not ready to approve.

DECIDE34 / 34

Frameworks, consistently

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The frameworks get applied to the projects that needed them least, and skipped for the ones that needed them most.

THE MOVE

Tools, not rules — adapt to context, but use them consistently, especially when the pitch is exciting.

Frameworks prevent reactive, emotional decisions.
